Shorting the Binance listing pump

The one hypothesis of roughly three thousand tested that survived its own backtest — and then failed the first clean out-of-sample test of it — reported here at the parameters that were not chosen by looking at the data. 115 listings over 1.89 years, priced on the perpetual actually shorted, with fees, spread, gap-through fills and the liquidation rule applied.

Out-of-sample replication, 2026-07-28: this failed

The rule below was run unchanged against 39 new Bybit spot listings the study had never touched, pre-registered before the run. Result: mean −3.72%, median −15.32%, win rate 38.5%. The modal trade did not weaken, it inverted — the typical outcome went from hitting the 15% target to hitting the 15% stop. If the true mean were the +1.99% this study measured on spot-priced events, a result this bad has a 0.55% chance of occurring.

The same run reproduced +2.06% on the 69 tokens that were in this study. Present on the tokens the study used, absent on tokens it never saw. That is the signature of a sample-specific result, not a mechanism.

What survives is only the narrower claim that Binance listings specifically behave this way — and that claim now has no clean evidence behind it at all, because this was the replication. Everything below stands as the record of what the Binance sample showed. Full pre-registration and result →

Live paper-trade monitor → the forward test described at the bottom of this page, running now on a VPS, updated every five minutes

This is not an established edge. The untuned rule reaches t 1.99 against a significance bar of 3.55, and on the subset that was never used to choose any parameter it reaches only 1.14. The direction is consistent and the mechanism is nameable, but the evidence does not clear the threshold this project holds everything else to.

There is also no clean holdout left. All 115 events were used to sweep the entry hour, pick the exits and repair filters. Forward data is the only uncontaminated test that remains.

The goal

Find a repeatable edge with a nameable mechanism, honest costs and a survivable tail — then size it so one bad event cannot end the account. Every earlier candidate failed on one of those: unmodelled swap, a spread wider than the signal, a units bug, or market beta wearing a hedge.

How long must a forward test run?

This is the number that decides what to do next. It follows from the measured dispersion of 14.6% per trade and an arrival rate of about 5.1 events a month — not from intuition.

Three months is about 15 events. That cannot confirm an edge — the mean would carry a ±3.8pp error bar. What it can do is detect a broken strategy: if the true win rate is 63% there is only a 3.5% chance of observing 40% or worse over 15 trades. So three months is a disaster detector, not a validation.

What is still wrong with this

  • No clean holdout exists All 115 events were used to search. A date split now cannot undo having looked. The trailing-exit test was given a properly sealed holdout because that idea was untouched — and it failed on DEV, so the holdout was never spent.
  • Four measurement bugs were found, and every fix helped The worst anchored the entry to midnight instead of the first traded hour, wrong by over six hours for 112 of 116 events. Another inherited a 12h perp cutoff from an earlier entry time. Each was demonstrable, but a run of favourable errors implies the undiscovered ones are probably favourable too.
  • Funding is not charged Average hold is 26.6h, about 3.3 funding periods on Gate. At a plausible 219%/yr rate the t falls to 1.5. A short often receives funding rather than paying, so this may be a credit — but it is unmeasured either way.
  • Spread is a guess 0.3% assumed; historical new-listing spreads are not published. This is the input most likely to be wrong and it moves the result directly.
  • Survivorship runs in your favour, which is unusual Tokens delisted after collapsing are absent, and those fell hardest. The measured effect is understated — the one bias in this project that flatters nothing.
  • Without the stop this ends the account 7 of 115 events moved far enough against the short to liquidate a 1× position. A short's loss is unbounded in a way a long's is not.
